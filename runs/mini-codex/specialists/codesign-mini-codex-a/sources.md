# Sources

Field: Organizational learning: how teams record decisions and revisit them

## Search focus and budget

Compared every K1–K5 and Q1–Q12 baseline answer with its excellent-answer criteria. The baseline already covers most procedural requirements; the largest omissions are named cases, empirical support, and demonstrated boundary conditions. Smaller omissions include explicit attribution limits in K5 and the practical limits of separating competing explanations in K3. Searches therefore targeted evidence behind the recommendations rather than assuming the baseline's uncited recommendations were established findings.

One round of five queries completed on 2026-11-13; 5/5 searches used. Opening returned sources and checking their original publication pages did not add search queries. No plan changes.

## Source log

### S1: Do Team and Individual Debriefs Enhance Performance? A Meta-Analysis
URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720812448394
Type: paper
Grade: A
Adds: Scott I. Tannenbaum and Christopher P. Cerasoli (online 2012; journal issue 2013) synthesize 46 samples, N = 2,136, reporting d = .67 versus controls and benefits of alignment between participants, review focus and measurement; the accessible publisher abstract supports these aggregate claims, not a causal claim about decision logs or any particular team's likely gain (K3, K5, Q8).

### S2: Documenting Architecture Decisions
URL: https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions
Type: practitioner-post
Grade: B
Adds: Michael Nygard (2011-11-15) reports early use across projects and positive context-recovery feedback from six to ten rotating developers; his short, numbered, repository-linked records retain superseded rationale and all consequences, while acknowledging stakeholder access concerns; this is a concrete adoption account, not comparative performance evidence (K1, K2, K5, Q1, Q2, Q11, Q12).

### S3: Ten Years of Talking About Machines
URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0170840606071933
Type: paper
Grade: A
Adds: Julian E. Orr (December 2006), revisiting his Xerox ethnography, corrects interpretations of technicians' choices: expensive machines could not simply be replaced at their discretion, and location/use could cause faults; accessible author notes also describe managerial resistance to studying actual tool use after acceptance testing, grounding context recovery and the distinction between adoption and use (K2, K5, Q3, Q4, Q8); scope is limited to the public abstract and notes, not the inaccessible body.

### S4: Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams
URL: https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Leadership/Edmondson_1999_Psychological_safety.pdf
Type: paper
Grade: A
Adds: Amy Edmondson (June 1999), in a multimethod study of 51 manufacturing-company teams, connects psychological safety with learning behavior and documents private concerns being withheld in low-learning teams; pp. 376–379 give contrasting cases and explicitly limit causal inference because of the cross-sectional, single-company design (K4, K5, Q9, Q10, Q8), without testing a specific dissent-channel intervention.

### S5: Postmortem Culture: Learning from Failure
URL: https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/
Type: book
Grade: B
Adds: Daniel Rogers, Murali Suriar, Sue Lueder, Pranjal Deo and Divya Sudhakar, with Gary O'Connor and Dave Rensin (2018), trace Google's 2014 satellite-disk-erasure incident to corrective work that reportedly reduced a similar incident's impact three years later; they describe searchable records, bug-tracked actions and incentives for completion, distinguishing the actual postmortem from a deliberately bad teaching example; this is practitioner case evidence, not a controlled effectiveness estimate (K3, K5, Q2, Q6, Q10, Q12).

## Coverage limits and exclusions

- No C sources logged. Generic summaries, promotional pages and anonymous discussions were excluded; they do not replace the originals above.
- S1's full-text PDF could not be retrieved; only claims checked in the publisher's accessible abstract are included. S3's accessible author notes support the narrow cases above; no detailed storytelling episode is inferred from secondary retellings.
- The five searches do not establish the incremental benefit of recording over conversation, long-term documentation cost, or universal thresholds for what deserves a record. S2's appreciation reports and S5's experience do not isolate causation.
- Direct experimental evidence on outcome/hindsight bias (Q7), concrete changes to governing assumptions or authority (Q5), event-trigger thresholds (Q11), and tested protections against powerful sponsors (K4, Q9, Q10) remain gaps. The baseline's recommendations on these points are not validated merely by their plausibility.
- Search stopped at the budget limit; the two-empty-round early-stop condition was not applicable. Only Step 5 was performed.
