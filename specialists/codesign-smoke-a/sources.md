# Sources: codesign-smoke-a

Field: Organizational learning: how teams record decisions and revisit them

Searches used: 5 of 5 (one round). Targets chosen from gaps in `baseline-answers.md` against the `Excellent answer:` lines in `tasks.md`: primary evidence on lessons-learned non-use (K3, Q8, where the baseline cited NASA audits only vaguely), evidence that reviews improve outcomes (research question 8, K2, Q10), field evidence on decision records in use (K1, Q3), the reversible versus hard-to-reverse distinction (K4, Q12, absent from the baseline), and survey evidence on project lessons practice (K3, Q8).

### S1: NASA: Better Mechanisms Needed for Sharing Lessons Learned (GAO-02-195)
URL: https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-02-195.pdf
Type: other
Grade: A
Adds: Primary survey data (U.S. GAO, 30 Jan 2002): although NASA rules required managers to review and apply lessons, they did not routinely identify, collect or share them; only 23% had ever entered a lesson into LLIS; managers said the system was hard to sift and gave no useful lessons, they were too busy to submit, and feared admitting mistakes would hurt their careers.

### S2: Review of NASA's Lessons Learned Information System (NASA OIG IG-12-012)
URL: https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ig-12-012.pdf
Type: other
Grade: A
Adds: Follow-up audit (NASA Office of Inspector General, 6 Mar 2012) showing the problem persisted a decade after GAO-02-195: program and project managers "rarely consult or contribute to LLIS" despite requirements, and no center except JPL contributed consistently from 2005 to 2010; evidence that mandates alone do not produce retrieval.

### S3: Do Team and Individual Debriefs Enhance Performance? A Meta-Analysis (Tannenbaum and Cerasoli, Human Factors 55(1), 2013)
URL: https://cebma.org/assets/Uploads/Tannenbaum-Cerasoli.pdf
Type: paper
Grade: A
Adds: Quantitative evidence for research question 8, missing from the baseline: across 46 samples (N = 2,136), debriefs such as after-action reviews improved performance about 20-25% over controls (d = .67); effects were larger when debriefs were structured, facilitated, and aligned (whole team reviewed, team performance measured).

### S4: Architecture Decision Records in Practice: An Action Research Study (Ahmeti, Linder, Groner, Wohlrab, ECSA 2024)
URL: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-70797-1_22
Type: paper
Grade: A
Adds: Field evidence for decision records in a real company (microservice system, 7 interviews, ADRs introduced and observed for three months): ADRs helped documentation culture, knowledge transfer and deciding what to document, and improved team cooperation; where records were stored strongly affected their usefulness; teams struggled to judge which decisions were architecturally significant and with shared components. Study materials on Zenodo (https://zenodo.org/records/11635100).

### S5: Amazon 2015 Letter to Shareholders (Jeff Bezos)
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312516530910/d168744dex991.htm
Type: other
Grade: B
Adds: Primary source for the Type 1 / Type 2 (one-way versus two-way door) distinction the baseline omitted: irreversible decisions get slow, deliberate process; reversible ones should be made fast by small groups; large organizations drift into using the heavy process for everything, causing slowness and risk aversion. Basis for scaling recording and review effort to reversibility (K4, Q12).

### S6: How Do Organizations Learn Lessons From Projects—And Do They? (Terry Williams, IEEE Transactions on Engineering Management 55(2), 2008)
URL: https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/output/417989/how-do-organizations-learn-lessons-from-projects-and-do-they
Type: paper
Grade: A
Adds: Peer-reviewed survey of project managers on actual lessons-learned practice and perceived success, plus empirical work on making project organizations learn; names structural blockers (temporary project organizations, project complexity). Headline figures reported by citing sources (for example, 96% rate lessons as important but only 8% feel they put enough effort in) were not verified against the paper itself and need checking at Step 6.
